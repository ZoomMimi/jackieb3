We left Eldean Shipyard early — a 6:36 a.m. departure, which tells you everything you need to know about how seriously Mike takes a weather window. The lake was cooperative and the crossing went smoothly, though I will say that having the Garmin radar up and tracking targets gave me a little peace of mind out there. You can see on the screen we had the MARPA system running with a couple of contacts painted — it's a good feeling knowing exactly where everyone is when you're out on open water.

As we made our way down the eastern shore of Lake Michigan, the scenery was absolutely stunning. The dunes along the shoreline are something else — great rolling hills of white sand tumbling right down to the water's edge, with dark green trees clinging to the tops. We spotted a beautiful tall brick lighthouse standing sentinel in front of the dunes and I took about a dozen photos trying to do it justice. I'm not sure I managed it, but you get the idea. The bluffs further along the shore were gorgeous too, bright white sand faces carved by the water over who knows how many thousands of years.

After about nine and a half hours underway we pulled into Harbor View Marina in Ludington, 75 nautical miles down the lake from Holland. Once we were tied up and settled, Mike and I headed into town to explore a little. We stumbled right into the Downtown Ludington Weekly Artisan & Farmers Market over at Legacy Plaza — live music, vendor tents everywhere, and a booth called the Cheese People that I may have spent a little too much time at. It was such a lively, happy scene. We grabbed drinks and sat outside at a table on the deck, just soaking it all in. Good company, good weather — you really can't ask for more than that.

The big entertainment of the evening, though, was the SS Badger. That enormous car ferry — "LMC" on the smokestack — is a Ludington landmark, and watching her come in and then depart right from the marina was quite a sight. She is just massive. We watched from the dock as she eased out of the channel with smoke trailing from the stack, with little fishing boats darting around her like minnows next to a whale. I took way too many pictures of her too, and I'm not apologizing for a single one.

What a wonderful stop. Ludington is charming, and I can see why so many Loopers love it. We are still in the middle of our Great Lakes stretch before heading back into Canadian waters for the North Channel — and days like this remind us exactly why we keep pointing the bow north.
