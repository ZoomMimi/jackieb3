What a perfect day to be back in Canada. We have been up here in the North Channel before — last year's Georgian Bay leg is something I'll never forget — but returning to these waters feels just as special the second time around. There is something about the Canadian Shield that just stops you in your tracks. The pink and grey granite, the pine trees growing right out of the rock, the water so clear and green you can see every boulder on the bottom. It never gets old.

We only ran about 16 nautical miles today, which tells you everything about the kind of day it was — we weren't in any hurry to get anywhere, and that was completely intentional.

The morning started with a wonderful Looper gathering. I loved looking around that room — pennants and burgees from yacht clubs all over the place lined every wall, and the group that filled the chairs and sofas was just as colorful. There is always something so comfortable about being in a room full of people who get it, who understand what it means to be out here doing this. We didn't want to leave.

But then the afternoon happened, and it did not disappoint. Once we got settled in our anchorage, the pink granite shoreline called to us. Mike was in the water with his snorkel and fins before I even had my shoes off — that man does not hesitate. I have to say the water was crystal clear and you could see right down to the rocky bottom. It looked like something from a nature documentary.

We took the dinghy out exploring and at one point Mike maneuvered it right up against the cliff face — I mean right up against it, close enough to touch the orange lichen on the rocks. I think I held my breath the whole time. He thought it was great fun. I may have aged a few years.

The real highlight for me, though, was watching two of our friends out on stand-up paddleboards. One was seated, paddling along, and the other was standing up like she'd been doing it her whole life, with the backdrop of pines and granite islands behind them and a whole fleet of sailboats at anchor. It was honestly one of the prettiest scenes I've seen on this whole trip. I just stood there and took way too many photos — you're welcome.

It was one of those days that reminds you exactly why you do this.
