We had a bit of a gray start to the day — the kind of morning where the fog settles right down onto the water and you can barely make out the boats in the next slip. I snapped a few photos at the dock early on and honestly the moody blue light was kind of beautiful, even if it wasn't exactly what you'd call "inspiring boating weather." Our neighbors in the slip were a lovely Carver named *Destiny*, and she looked perfectly at home in the mist.

We were so happy to have our friends Betsy and Dave along for this leg. What a treat to share the Jackie B III with good company! We made our way from Holland down to Grand Haven — just under 20 nautical miles, which sounds quick on paper, and it was — but the fog on the water made Mike keep a careful eye on things the whole way. I grabbed a shot from inside the cabin looking out at that grey, soupy horizon and all I can say is thank goodness for electronics. The water was a gorgeous blue-green even under all those clouds, and the flags up on the bow were flapping along cheerfully like they hadn't gotten the memo about the weather.

One little detail I'll mention for fellow Loopers who might be heading through Michigan state harbors — they hand you a little slip of paper when you check in with all the rules, and yes, the bathroom code is on there. (I photographed mine so I wouldn't forget it. Don't judge me.)

We made it to Grand Haven safe and sound, and the four of us managed a happy dockside selfie in spite of the overcast skies. You can't help but smile when you're back on the water in Canada-adjacent Great Lakes territory — this whole return trip to the North Channel area has us feeling like we've been given a wonderful gift of getting to do it all over again. So glad Betsy and Dave got to be part of it.
