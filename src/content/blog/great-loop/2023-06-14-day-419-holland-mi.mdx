We got a very early start — or at least we *tried* to. The first photo I took that morning is basically completely black, which tells you everything you need to know about what time we were up and moving around the dock. So much for the glamorous boating life!

By the time we could actually see anything, the marina was wrapped in a beautiful blue pre-dawn fog. We had some wonderful friends joining us for this leg of the adventure, and we snapped a few shots of the dock before pulling out. You can see *Destiny* tied up right next to us in the early morning light — the whole marina had that quiet, hushed feeling you only get when you're out before most sensible people have had their first cup of coffee.

The run from Eldean Shipyard down to Grand Haven was just under 20 miles and took us less than two hours, which was a good thing because the fog never really lifted. Mike kept his eyes fixed on the water the whole way — you can see him at the helm scanning the horizon, which at that point was basically just a wall of gray. The water was actually a beautiful shade of blue-green despite the murky sky overhead, but our seat cushions got more of the view than I did since I was huddled inside staying warm. It was around 50 degrees — in July, bless Michigan's heart.

We're heading back to Canada — specifically up through the North Channel in Ontario — and Grand Haven is our staging point. We've been to Canadian waters before during the Loop, so this feels a little like returning to visit an old friend. We are so excited to get back up there.

Once we got tied up, the marina handed us our slip paperwork with the usual rules and the bathroom code handwritten at the bottom. (3757, in case you were wondering. I always photograph those because I *always* forget them five minutes later.) The State of Michigan, it turns out, is not responsible for your boating equipment. Good to know.

Our friends who joined us for this leg took a group photo on the dock with the Jackie B III behind us — it's a little sideways but we're all smiling, which is really what counts. It is always more fun when you have good company for the ride.
