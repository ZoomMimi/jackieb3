What a day! We left Myrtle Beach around 10:45 this morning and made our way up the ICW toward Southport, North Carolina — 38.6 nautical miles and about five hours of some of the prettiest coastal scenery you can imagine. The water was calm, the clouds were dramatic in that beautiful way they get along the Carolina coast, and Mike had us threading bridges like a pro. We went through so many — Barefoot Resort, Robert Edge Parkway, Sea Mountain Highway, Highway 17, Sunset Boulevard, Causeway Drive, Long Beach Road — honestly I lost count after a while and just started enjoying the ride.

The views out the back of the Jackie B III were gorgeous. Low sandy islands, marsh grass, green channel buoys, pine trees lining the shore — this stretch of the ICW has a wildness to it that I really love. There were moments where the water opened up wide and you could see the barrier islands stretching out with white-capped ocean surf just beyond the dunes. I may have taken about forty photos of essentially the same thing. I regret nothing.

We pulled into Morningstar Marinas in Southport and got settled in. Colby was our dock staff and got us squared away right away — those little marina maps with the handwritten wifi password and bathroom code are such a thing of beauty after a long run. The wifi password was "southport," which is just perfect.

Southport itself is charming. We walked around a bit and found a wonderful porch with a water view framed by live oaks — the kind of scene that stops you in your tracks. Shrimp boats were working the channel right in front of us, which felt so authentically North Carolina. One even came gliding past right at dusk, nets all rigged out, looking like it belonged on a postcard.

We had a fun group gathering on a porch with a "C-Life Southport, NC" sign — good people, good laughs, and honestly the best kind of welcome to a new town.

Now — I have to back up just a little, because the wristbands. The night before we left Myrtle Beach, Mike and I caught a show at House of Blues. The red-lit photos of his Garmin tell the whole story: heart rate up, steps logged, and those white House of Blues wristbands firmly in place. Apparently my Garmin thinks that counts as a recovery day. I'm not arguing with it.
