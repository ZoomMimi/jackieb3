We are back in Canada — and honestly, it feels like coming home. The North Channel of Lake Huron is one of those places that just gets into your soul, and returning to these waters in July 2023 has us every bit as awestruck as we were on our first visit during the Loop. Some things you don't mind seeing twice. Or three times, for that matter.

We got underway just after 10 in the morning, the air a pleasant 18 or 19 degrees Celsius — which as Americans we are still doing the mental math on, but we're pretty sure it was lovely. The wind was light out of the south-southeast, the water was calm, and we covered about 16 nautical miles in just over two hours. Easy cruising through some of the most beautiful scenery on earth, which I realize I say a lot, but up here I really mean it.

The star of the day was the dinghy ride we did to poke around the granite islands up close. Photos just do not do justice to the sheer scale of these rock faces — some of them rise straight out of the water in great slabs of grey and pink, smoothed by glaciers thousands of years ago and now streaked with years of waterline staining. The pine trees somehow find a way to grow right out of the cracks, which I find both inspiring and a little humbling. I can barely keep a houseplant alive.

Mike was in full explorer mode, nosing us through narrow little passages between the islands where the water goes perfectly still and the reflections of the trees look like a mirror. We spotted a lone sailboat tucked in near the rocks — the only other sign of humanity for miles. White water lilies were blooming along one quieter shoreline, and a bird skimmed low across the water just as I snapped a photo. I cannot promise the bird made it into the frame, but I tried.

We also had a quiet moment on the boat that I will just say was nice. Mike stole a kiss — or maybe I stole it, I honestly can't remember — and someone was thoughtful enough to photograph the whole thing from multiple angles. I am surrounded by a very supportive crew.

The Canadian flag flies from our stern up here, and it fits. What a place.
