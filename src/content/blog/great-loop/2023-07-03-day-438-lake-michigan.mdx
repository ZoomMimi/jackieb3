What a day on Lake Michigan! We left Betsie Lake at 8:44 in the morning with light winds out of the north — perfect conditions — and made our way about 25 miles out onto the lake before anchoring around noon. The water temperature was a cool 56 degrees (that's 15.9°C for our Canadian friends, since we are back in that Great Lakes neighborhood again!). Yes, we are back in Canadian waters for a return visit this summer, and I have to say the Great Lakes never get old.

Before we left Betsie Lake, we had a chance to explore a lovely lighthouse right there on shore. Mike and I were both grinning ear to ear taking our selfie in front of it — that tall white tower against a perfectly blue sky is just something. I tried to get an artistic straight-up shot from the base, which I think turned out rather nicely, even if I was the only one impressed by my photography skills.

We went inside and climbed the spiral staircase — one of those gorgeous old cast-iron spirals with the beautiful lattice-work treads. I'll be honest, looking straight down through those open steps was not my favorite moment. I had a death grip on the railing and I'm pretty sure the expression on my face said it all. But we made it to the top, and oh my goodness, was it worth it. The view from the gallery was absolutely breathtaking — white sand curving along the shoreline, the water shifting from pale green near the beach to the most vivid turquoise out toward the horizon. You could see for miles in every direction. We even spotted a couple of tiny figures on the beach way below us that looked like ants. Mike leaned over and I grabbed the railing again.

On our way in, we passed an old weathered dock that had been completely taken over by birds — cormorants and gulls perched on every beam and railing, with more swooping around in the air. It looked like something out of a nature documentary. The water around it was that same unbelievable shade of green.

We also wandered past some old farm equipment left along a sandy path in the trees — rusted iron wheels and rakes that looked like they hadn't moved in a century. It was a sweet little reminder that people lived and worked this land long before any of us showed up with our boats.

After we anchored for the afternoon, a diver appeared right alongside the Jackie B III, floating in that impossibly clear turquoise water. Looking straight down from the deck at that diver was almost surreal — the water was so clear it felt like looking through glass. A little while later a snorkeler was gliding through the same water nearby, completely at home.

And then the evening gave us the best gift of all. A great big orange moon rose up over the tree line and reflected in the water — deep red-orange, almost like it was on fire. I tried to get a good photo and I think I halfway succeeded. You may need to squint a little. It was the kind of moon that makes you just stand quietly and stare.

What a wonderful day to be on the water.
