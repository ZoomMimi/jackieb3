We are back in Canada, and I have to say — this place does not disappoint the second time around. After doing this stretch of the North Channel during our original Loop, we knew we wanted to return, and here we are. Some places just stay with you.

We got underway a little before 10 this morning in cool, pleasant weather — around 62°F with a light westerly breeze. The shoreline running past us as we made our way through the channel was just stunning. Those ancient Canadian Shield rock faces drop straight into the most impossibly green water, with evergreens crowding right to the edge. I honestly could have just stood on the bow and stared all morning.

![](rocky shoreline with green water)

We made a fuel stop along the way — and I'll just note that buying diesel by the litre is one of those things that sounds like a better deal until you do the math. The pump photo says it all. Welcome back to Canada!

After fueling we motored a short distance to our anchorage in a breathtaking protected cove — the kind of place that looks like it was drawn by someone who had never actually seen anything ugly. The water inside was a deep jewel green, absolutely calm, ringed by pink granite cliffs and thick stands of pine. Several sailboats were already swinging on the hook when we arrived, and by the time we settled in the place was quite the gathering. Looking down at all those masts from up on the rocks later was really something.

![](aerial view of cove with sailboats at anchor)

Speaking of up on the rocks — that was the afternoon's adventure. We dinghied ashore (I spotted a couple of other inflatables ferrying people in, so we were in good company) and started hiking up through the forest. The trail winds through some beautiful old cedars and pines, and someone long ago built a rather rustic log ladder structure to get up the steeper sections. Mike, being Mike, practically scrambled up it like a squirrel. I took a slightly more dignified approach. Slightly.

![](log ladder structure in the woods)

Once we got up on the open granite, the views just opened up and kept going. Looking straight down into that cove full of boats was worth every step of the climb. And looking out the other direction you could see water all the way to the horizon — the North Channel stretching out big and silver in the afternoon light. At one point the sun had this beautiful halo ring around it, the kind that makes you stop mid-sentence and just point.

![](sun halo over the treetops and cove)

We were up there with two friends who had joined us for this part of the adventure, and we took full advantage of the photo opportunities — upside-down selfies on the cliff edge included. Why not? You're on top of a granite bluff in Ontario. Normal rules don't apply.

The wild blueberries growing right out of the cracks in the rock were a lovely little bonus. Tiny, perfectly ripe, absolutely delicious. Canada's version of trail mix, I suppose.

![](wild blueberry on the rock)

What a day. This is exactly why we came back.
