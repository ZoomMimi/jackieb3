Back in Michigan for a quick stop on our return from Canada — and what a stop it turned out to be! We made the run down to Beaver Island, about 32 nautical miles in just under four hours, arriving in the early afternoon with a nice breeze out of the west-northwest. The weather was perfect for the crossing.

But the real story of this day wasn't the passage at all. It was the evening.

Someone on the island apparently knew something we didn't, because by sunset there were little clusters of people making their way out to the beach — and once that sun started dropping toward the horizon, it was very clear why. Mike and I grabbed our fleeces (yes, fleeces — it's July in northern Michigan, people!) and headed out to find a spot on the sand dunes.

What followed was honestly one of the most beautiful sunsets I have ever witnessed. The sky turned every shade of orange and gold you can imagine, and that sun — a perfect glowing red-orange ball — just slowly melted into the water. I took about a hundred photos trying to capture it — the sky reflected in the water was just as gorgeous as the real thing.

We sat back against the dunes and just watched, along with a handful of other beachgoers who had the good sense to be right there with us. Nobody said much. There wasn't really anything to say.

After all the time we spent in the North Channel on this return visit to Canada — which was just as breathtaking as we remembered from 2022 — I wasn't sure anything could top it. But Canada, Michigan was more than happy to put up a fight.

What a gift this day was.

"From the rising of the sun to the place where it sets, the name of the Lord is to be praised."
