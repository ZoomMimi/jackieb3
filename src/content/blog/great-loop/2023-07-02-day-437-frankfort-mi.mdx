What a lovely day in Frankfort, Michigan! We are not in Canada yet — that leg is still ahead of us — but days like this remind us why the Great Lakes region keeps pulling us back.

We spent part of the afternoon at the Cabbage Shed in Elberta, just across the bay from Frankfort. I always love a place with a good story, and this one has a great one. I read the whole history right off the menu insert — the building goes all the way back to 1867 when a man named George A. Douglas built the town's first general merchandise store on that very site. It changed hands a few times over the years, became a farmers' cooperative warehouse stacked floor to ceiling with cabbage in the 1920s (hence the name!), and eventually became the restaurant and pub it is today. Their slogan on the menu made me laugh: *"We were here before you were born!"* Hard to argue with that.

The drink menu was equally entertaining. I spotted the Negroni Orange Fizz — made with award-winning Ironfish Gin, Aperol, sweet vermouth, and soda with a hint of orange — and I was sold. Doesn't it look beautiful sitting there on the rail in the afternoon sun? The Betsie Bay Pain Killer description asked, "Too much paddling today?" — I mean, we came by boat, so I figured that counted. We also ordered what appeared to be a warm cookie skillet with ice cream and hot fudge that was completely over the top in the best possible way. I am not sorry at all.

The real show, though, came later in the evening. The moon rising over the water was absolutely stunning — one of those scenes where you just stop talking and stare. The moonlight laid a shimmering golden path right across the lake, and we walked down to the beach to take it all in. There were a few other folks out there doing the same thing, just sitting in the sand, looking up. Mike and I managed a selfie that came out completely upside down, which feels about right for us. 😄

Some nights on this trip just feel like pure gift. This was one of them.
