What a fun little detour before we make our way back into Canada! Mackinac Island is one of those places that just never gets old, and stepping off the ferry and onto a street with zero cars — only horses and bicycles — has a way of instantly resetting your soul.

We started the day doing what any sensible boaters do when they find themselves on a gorgeous island: we went to explore the rocky shoreline. And I do mean *rocky.* The limestone formations along the water's edge are something else entirely — full of holes and caves carved out by centuries of waves. Mike, of course, could not simply admire them from a safe and reasonable distance. Nope. Within minutes he was scaling the face of those rocks in his sandals — sandals! — while I stood below trying to decide whether to take a photo or call for backup. He made it, naturally, arm raised in triumph. I'll admit it was impressive. I'll also admit I was not about to follow him up there.

We took about forty-seven selfies against those beautiful pitted limestone bluffs — which is what you do when the light is that good and the backdrop is that dramatic. The golden evening glow on the rock and water was just stunning.

Earlier in the day I spotted the most entertaining sight: a horse-drawn wagon rolling right past the Mackinac Island State Harbor sign, absolutely stacked with Amazon boxes. I mean *stacked.* No cars on this island, so that's how everything gets delivered — by horse. There was something wonderfully absurd about a team of handsome dark horses hauling what was clearly someone's online shopping haul through this picture-perfect Victorian streetscape. Only on Mackinac.

We had lunch at the 906 Club — I had an iced coffee that was exactly what I needed, and we sat outside under the red umbrellas and just soaked it all in. Later, dinner was a treat — beautiful plates, including what looked like a gorgeous tuna tartare starter and a prime rib with an arugula and goat cheese salad that did not disappoint. The view from the restaurant out over the harbor — flags flying, the marina below, all those colorful buildings — was as good as the food.

This trip back through this part of Michigan before we slip back across into Canada has been a wonderful reminder of how much there is to love right here in the Great Lakes. We are so blessed to get to see it all again from the water.
