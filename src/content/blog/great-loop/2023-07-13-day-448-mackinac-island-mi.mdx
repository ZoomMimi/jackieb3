Back in Canada! We were so excited to be returning to Canadian waters — this trip to the North Channel has been on our wish list since we first fell in love with this part of the world during our Loop. We left the Mackinac Island State Dock early, around 6:25 in the morning, with cool temperatures and a light breeze out of the north-northwest. It was a beautiful morning to be on the water.

We hadn't been underway very long when we spotted a large ship in the distance. At first I thought maybe I was imagining things — what would a cruise ship be doing out here? But as it got closer there was no mistaking it. It was a Viking cruise ship, and what a sight it was! I took about a hundred photos trying to capture just how massive and beautiful it looked gliding across Lake Huron. It is hard to believe something that big can navigate these waters. We also passed a tugboat pushing a barge along a wooded shoreline — quite the contrast in vessel sizes out there on the lake that day!

We made a stop mid-morning before continuing east toward Canadian waters, which gave Mike and me a chance to take care of some very important business — putting up our yellow quarantine flag. You know you're a seasoned cruiser when you know exactly where that flag is stowed. Well, let's just say it took us a few minutes to locate it, and there may have been a blue bowl involved in the operation. But we got it up and flying proudly before we crossed into Canada. We always love this little tradition — there is something about hoisting that yellow flag that makes the whole adventure feel official.

By the time we arrived at our anchorage late in the afternoon, we had covered 76.7 nautical miles in just over nine hours. Not bad for a day's work. The water on Lake Huron was a gorgeous deep blue, the skies were clear, and we couldn't have asked for a better day to make the passage. Canada, here we come — again!
