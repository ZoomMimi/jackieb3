What a fun morning on the water. Mike and I got underway just before 11:00 with a nice easterly breeze keeping things comfortable, and the ICW did not disappoint. Thirty-two and a half miles of Florida at its finest — palm trees, sandy little beaches, beautiful waterfront homes with their docks and boat lifts tucked right up to the water's edge.

One of the highlights of the whole run was passing our friend Tracy's dock. There she was, standing right on her dock in her backyard, camera in hand, snapping photos of us as we cruised by. We'd stayed with her and her husband several nights during our time in the area, and it was such a sweet send-off seeing her out there waving us along. We may have honked the horn. Just once. Or twice.

The scenery along this stretch was gorgeous — open water views framed by sabal palms, wide sandy shores, and Spanish moss draped over the live oaks right down to the water. We passed under several bridges on our way into Daytona Beach, including the Dunlawton and the Main Street Bridge, which always feel like little milestones ticking by on the Loop.

By the time we tied up that afternoon the sky had put on quite a show. Those sunset clouds were absolutely dramatic — big towering cumulonimbus formations lit up from behind in deep orange and pink, glowing like something out of a painting. I took way too many photos and they still don't do it justice.

We also discovered that wherever we were tied up had the most creative men's room fixture I have ever seen in my life. A beer keg. Repurposed as a urinal. I'm told it was quite impressive. I'll take everyone's word for it.

And our little night heron friend perched right at the dock edge after dark, completely unbothered by us. These birds have zero concept of personal space and I love them for it.
