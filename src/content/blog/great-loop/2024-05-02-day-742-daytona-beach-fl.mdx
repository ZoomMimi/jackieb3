What a sky we had waiting for us in Daytona Beach!

Mike and I left our starting point around 10:51 in the morning with a gentle easterly breeze — about 6 knots — and honestly pretty perfect conditions for the run up the ICW. Thirty-two and a half nautical miles, a handful of bridges to watch for, and we were tied up by mid-afternoon. Not a bad day's work.

The scenery along the way did not disappoint. Palm trees, sandy little shores, covered docks with Adirondack chairs in that perfect shade of turquoise — the kind of waterfront property that makes you wonder how anyone ever goes inside. We passed some lovely private docks along the way, and I spotted someone out on one of them taking a photo right back at us. I completely understand the impulse.

Now, I have to tell you about the men's room. I cannot not tell you about the men's room. Mike pointed it out to me and I had to go see for myself — the urinal was a repurposed beer keg mounted right there on the wall. I mean, whoever thought of that deserves some kind of award. Practically speaking, I appreciate that it was spotless. Decoratively speaking, it is the most on-brand restroom fixture I have ever seen in a marina town. Only in Florida.

The real showstopper, though, was the evening. We had barely settled in when the sky started doing things. Big, dramatic, anvil-topped storm clouds lit up from behind in the most outrageous shades of orange and pink — the kind that look like they're on fire from the inside. I stood on the dock and just kept taking pictures because I couldn't quite believe what I was seeing. Even as the light faded and the clouds shifted into long, dark silhouettes against a golden horizon, it kept going. A little night heron perched right at the edge of the dock in the dark — perfectly still, perfectly unbothered — while I was still out there clicking away.

Some sunsets you sit back and appreciate. This one made you stand up.
