We are back in Canada — well, almost. Actually today we were making our way *back* toward the U.S. side after our return visit to the North Channel, and what a day it turned out to be. We left Scammon Cove early, around 6:39 in the morning, coffees in hand (you can see our trusty travel mugs lined up on the console — mine in red, Mike's in gray). The water out there was anything but friendly. The crossing was choppy and gray, with swells that made for a lumpy 79-plus nautical miles over about seven and a half hours. I will be honest — there were stretches where I was very grateful for the enclosure on the upper helm. You just hold your coffee tight and remind yourself that it will get better.

One of the first orders of business was getting ourselves cleared back into the United States. Mike submitted our CBP entry through the app and we watched that little "Status: Pending" screen for a while. There is something mildly nerve-wracking about that spinning status bar even when you have done absolutely nothing wrong. Request #625959, if you are curious. It eventually cleared and we were good to go.

We ended up at St. Ignace Marina, which was a very welcome sight after the crossing. They hand you a little slip of paper with the bathroom code (341, for anyone keeping score) and the wifi password, and somehow that small gesture of hospitality feels like a warm hug after a long day on the water.

Now — Mike being Mike — he did not exactly sit down and relax once we were tied up. He pulled one of the engine air filters right there on the dock. I have to say, looking at the thing, it was pretty well done. The foam seal around the bottom was cracked and crumbling apart. Good catch, honestly. That is the kind of maintenance that is a lot easier to deal with at a dock than out in the middle of Lake Huron.

But the real treat of the afternoon was the boat tied up nearby. It was a gorgeous vintage Chris-Craft — all warm mahogany and gleaming chrome, with the most beautiful stained-glass porthole detail I think I have ever seen on a boat. Just stunning. She even had a framed first-place award from the Les Cheneaux Islands Antique Wooden Boat Show — 44th show, August 13, 2022, first place in the Lapstrake Skiff category for a boat named *Barrel Back*. Somebody has put an enormous amount of love into that vessel and it absolutely shows. The reflection in the still water next to the dock was almost as pretty as the boat itself. We could have stood there admiring her all afternoon.

Grateful to be safely tied up at St. Ignace Marina tonight. The Volvo Penta engines ran beautifully all day — both showing good temperatures and pressures the whole crossing — and that is always something to be thankful for.
