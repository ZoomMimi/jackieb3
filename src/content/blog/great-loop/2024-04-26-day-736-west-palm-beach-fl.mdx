What a day to be on the water — 62.8 nautical miles down the ICW, underway most of the day, taking it all in as we made our way to Palm Beach Yacht Center.

I have to say, West Palm Beach has to be one of the most jaw-dropping stretches of the whole Loop when it comes to boats. And I don't mean boats like the Jackie B III — I mean the kind of boats that have their own boats. We passed row after row of megayachts, and I kept reaching for my camera. One beauty called *Top Five II* was lined up with a whole fleet of gleaming white superyachts, all of them so perfectly polished you could practically do your hair in their reflection. Then we came across *Mary A* moored alongside a massive dark-hulled ship — the kind that carries a full-size tender on its side deck like it's a carry-on bag. I zoomed in and sure enough, there was a rigid inflatable strapped to the hull several stories up. I just kept thinking: our whole boat could fit inside that thing's garage.

We also spotted *Lady Barbara* docked along a lovely canal lined with palm trees — which Mike thought was very appropriate given the company we keep on this trip. She's a gorgeous white motor yacht, right at home in front of one of those Mediterranean-style waterfront estates.

The anchorage had a couple of catamarans swinging peacefully against a backdrop of green mangroves and blue sky — a nice reminder that not every boat around here costs nine figures.

That evening we walked over to the Nauti Dawg Marina Cafe, right there at Lighthouse Point Marina. That glowing yellow lab with the life ring on the sign? Honestly, I love him. The whole place has that perfect marina-casual vibe, and it was just the right way to cap the day. Three of us smiled for a photo in front of the sign and I'm pretty sure we were all still thinking about those megayachts.

The sunset from the dock was absolutely on fire — orange and gold and pink all at once, the palm tree silhouettes dark against it. No filter needed. No filter could even help, really.
