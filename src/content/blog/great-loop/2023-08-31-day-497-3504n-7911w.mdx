Well, the Jackie B III was staying put today, and honestly I think we all needed a land day. The GPS puts us near 35°N, 79°W, which for those keeping score at home is squarely in North Carolina — not exactly the North Channel, but sometimes life pulls you back to shore for a bit, and you just go with it.

We visited a bamboo grove that I can only describe as otherworldly. The stalks were absolutely enormous — some of them as thick as my forearm — and they lean into each other at every angle, creating this dense, cathedral-like canopy overhead. I kept looking up and thinking it felt more like Southeast Asia than the American South. There was a little sign posted at the entrance that said "Please Do Not Cut Bamboo," which I thought was very reasonable advice given how magnificent the whole thing was. Mike and I wandered in and just stood there for a minute, kind of speechless. A picnic table sat at the edge of the grove, which seemed like exactly the right place to sit and take it all in. I wish I had better words for it — you really have to stand inside it to understand.

The afternoon brought a completely different kind of adventure: yard work with the grandkids. And I mean that in the best possible way. There were logs to be moved, and those little ones were not about to be left out. The youngest — in his little teal dinosaur shirt — hauled a chunk of wood that was nearly as big as he was. The older two were right in the thick of it alongside the men, carrying logs back and forth like they'd been doing it their whole lives. I stood there watching and just laughed. There is something so sweet about little ones who want to work right alongside the grown-ups. Mike was right in the middle of it all, doing what he does.

The littlest one also spent some time toddling around a wooden structure nearby — I couldn't quite tell what it was, maybe an old farm display of some kind — but he was perfectly happy just exploring on his own, taking in the world at his own pace. That teal dinosaur shirt showing up in photo after photo made me smile every time.

Not every day has to be a big nautical adventure. Sometimes the best ones involve bamboo and babies hauling logs.
