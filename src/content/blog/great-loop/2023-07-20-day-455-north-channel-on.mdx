We are back in Canada, and the North Channel is just as spectacular as we remembered from our Georgian Bay leg last year. I will say, coming back to these waters felt a little like running into an old friend — that same dramatic Canadian Shield scenery, those massive pale granite cliffs rising straight out of the water with the pines clinging to every ledge. It never gets old.

Today was a short one on the water — only 2.7 nautical miles and less than an hour underway, which honestly might be a record for us. We left just after 11:30 in the morning with a light breeze out of the southeast and temperatures in the upper 60s, and we were tucked into our new anchorage just after noon. Short day, but the destination was worth every minute of that half-hour cruise.

The anchorage itself was gorgeous — a sheltered little cove ringed by those beautiful layered rock faces with tall pines right to the edge, the water so calm it looked like a mirror. A handful of sailboats were already settled in, their masts reflected perfectly in the still water. I could have just sat on the bow all afternoon staring at it.

But the real show started in the evening. The skies began to build into something dramatic — deep blue-gray clouds rolling and stacking up above the tree line, with just a sliver of golden light low on the horizon underneath. Mike and I headed out in the dinghy to get a better look at the anchorage from the water, and I have to say I may have taken approximately forty-seven photos of the same view. I regret nothing. The storm clouds reflected in the completely glassy water were almost too beautiful to believe.

And then there was the Jackie B III herself, all lit up at the dock with her blue underwater lights glowing in the dark. I don't care how many times I see those blue lights reflected in a calm anchorage — it just never looks real. Like something out of a dream.

Earlier in the day we had a truly memorable meal — Mike went full caveman on a tomahawk steak that arrived on a wooden board looking like something out of a Viking feast. At some point the fork and knife became entirely optional. I'm not going to say anything more except that the bone came back to the table very, very clean, and I have photographic evidence.

What a day to be back in Canada.
