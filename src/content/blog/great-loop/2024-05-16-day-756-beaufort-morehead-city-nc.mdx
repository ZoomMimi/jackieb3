What a day. An early alarm, a thermos of coffee, and one of the most stunning sunrises we've seen in a long time greeted us as we pulled away just after 6:30 this morning. The sky was all gold and pewter — clouds lit up from the inside like a lantern — with the sun barely clearing the horizon right over the bow. I stood there thinking I should be doing something useful and instead just stared. Mike was already focused on getting us underway, which is probably why we make a good team.

We covered nearly 90 miles today in just under six hours of running, which honestly felt like nothing because the water was so cooperative. The first leg took us through the ICW, ticking past markers and the Carolina Beach Road Bridge before we opened up into the bigger water of the second leg. That's when the scenery really changed.

Out on the open water, the sky went full golden haze — the kind where the sun is completely wrapped in clouds but still pouring light everywhere. You could just barely make out structures on the horizon. Stunning in a slightly eerie, end-of-days sort of way. (Don't worry, we made it.)

Along the way we passed a tug pushing a massive barge — the kind of thing that makes you very glad you're not in its path and also very glad you have radar. We gave them plenty of room.

As we came up the coast we got beautiful views of the barrier island beaches — white sand, dune grass, a red-roofed building with a big American flag flying proud. People dotted the beach with their chairs and umbrellas, completely unaware we were slowly cruising by admiring their afternoon. Must be nice. (It was also nice for us, honestly.)

Then came the shrimp boats. A whole fleet of them tied up together, red hulls bright against the blue sky, outriggers reaching in every direction. Magnificent in that very working-waterfront way. Our AGLCA and U.S. Army flags were flying on the bow — felt appropriate to be arriving somewhere that real work gets done.

We ended the day tied up in Beaufort/Morehead City, and the sunset delivered in a big way. Mike and I were grinning like idiots, lying right on the bow to get the best angle. The sky went pure orange and red behind a long bridge pier, just absolutely on fire. Matching red sweatshirts, matching smiles. A day done right.

Tomorrow is the last one.
