What a morning to push off from Boca Chita. The sunrise over the water was absolutely ridiculous — the kind where you just stand on the grass with your jaw open and keep taking photos even though you know none of them will do it justice. I took about a dozen anyway. The sky went from soft pink and lavender to this full-on blaze of orange as the sun dropped right between the trees at the horizon. And then — just when I thought it couldn't get any better — I turned around and there was the moon still hanging up in the blue behind us. I mean, come on.

We got underway around 7:48 and headed north out of Biscayne Bay. It was a gorgeous morning on the water — low 70s, light breeze out of the ENE, the kind of conditions that make you forget you've been at this for 735 days. We passed through Miami and I grabbed some shots of the downtown skyline from the water. The big Ferris wheel and the Hard Rock Cafe were right there front and center. It never gets old seeing a city from the water — you get a completely different perspective than driving through.

But the real show came as we made our way north and hit the megayacht zone. Oh my. These are not boats. These are floating apartment buildings. One right after another, lined up at the docks like they were parked there just for us to gawk at. We spotted *Leverage*, *Ablona* — which had an actual helicopter sitting on its top deck, not kidding — *Amadeus*, *Bookends*, and a handful of others whose names I couldn't quite catch. One had its side garage door wide open with a whole tender and what looked like a jet ski just tucked inside like it was nothing. Mike and I just kept looking at each other. The Jackie B III is a perfectly lovely boat, but she would have fit inside one of these things with room to spare.

We covered 47.4 miles in about 7 hours and tied up in the Fort Lauderdale area by mid-afternoon. A great day on the water — and apparently a great day for megayacht spotting too!
