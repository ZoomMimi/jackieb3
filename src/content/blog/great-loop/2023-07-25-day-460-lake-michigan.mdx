What a day on Lake Michigan! We left St. Ignace Municipal Marina mid-morning under perfect conditions — barely a breath of wind and a comfortable temperature in the low 60s. It felt so good to be back on the Great Lakes, and yes, I'll say it again — we are back in Canada-adjacent waters for this little bonus loop adventure, and the lakes never disappoint.

The big highlight of the day was our stop at White Shoal Lighthouse. If you've never seen White Shoal, let me tell you — it is something else. That distinctive candy-striped tower rising out of Lake Michigan with nothing but turquoise water all around it is just stunning. Mike had the drone up and got a beautiful aerial shot of the tender tied off nearby, the water so clear and brilliant blue-green you'd think you were looking at the Caribbean. I'm not exaggerating. Lake Michigan has no business looking that beautiful.

We were lucky enough to get inside the lighthouse, which is in active restoration. The caretaker on site — a hard-hat-wearing fellow who clearly knows every inch of that tower — gave us a look around that most people never get. The interior is, shall we say, a work in progress. Peeling paint, crumbling walls, old iron staircases — and then you step outside onto the gallery and the view takes your breath away. The Fresnel lens is long gone (a posted article inside explained the whole complicated history of removing and relocating it), but what remains is still magnificent.

Oh, and there's a skeleton propped in one of the windows wearing a White Shoal Light t-shirt. I'm not sure if he's meant to be a greeter or a warning. Either way, he gave me a start.

Mike and I posed with the life ring on the dock — it reads "White Shoal Light" — which seemed like the right souvenir photo when you can't actually take anything home. There were a few other folks visiting the same day, all of us marveling at this incredible structure sitting completely alone in the middle of Lake Michigan, constructed in 1910 and still standing strong. The walls at the base are massive and red — almost makes it look like it's built from a different century's ambitions entirely. Which I suppose it is.

After we left White Shoal, we made our way south and east to Petoskey Municipal Marina on Little Traverse Bay. I'd stretched out on the bow cushion to enjoy the ride — the sun was warm, the water calm, and I was not about to feel guilty about it for one second.

The Jackie B III was all lit up in the slip that evening with her underwater lights glowing blue into the water. It made for a stunning end to a full and beautiful day. We watched the marina lights shimmer pink across the water after dark and called it a perfect Tuesday on Lake Michigan.
