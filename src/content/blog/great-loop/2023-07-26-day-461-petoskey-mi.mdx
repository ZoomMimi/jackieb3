Back on the American side — at least for a night — and Petoskey, Michigan did not disappoint. We had just wrapped up our return visit to Canadian waters, and I have to say, coming back through the North Channel reminded us all over again why we fell so hard for that part of the world the first time. But Petoskey has its own charm, and we were glad to be here.

The first thing that caught my eye when we got off the boat and walked the waterfront was a wonderful tall clock tower — deep red ironwork with Roman numerals on the face and bells hanging right alongside it. I took about fifteen photos of it. The whole waterfront area was beautifully done, with patterned brick walkways and lovely landscaping, and the clock tower anchored it all perfectly.

We got the bikes out and explored a bit. I stopped for a photo at a railroad crossing — because of course I did — and I have to say, between navigating the tracks and watching for trains, it was a full adventure. The town has a great feel to it, the kind of place where you want to slow down and just wander.

The highlight of the evening though was dinner with two friends who joined us. We found the most wonderful restaurant — think chandeliers, lace curtains, gorgeous tablecloths, and cocktails that were almost too pretty to drink. Almost. Those martinis were something else, I can tell you that. We ended up with enough photos of them that you'd think we'd never seen a cocktail before. The four of us had such a good time sitting out on the porch, laughing and catching up. It was one of those evenings that you want to bottle up and keep.

And then, the sunset. Oh my goodness. The sun was so deeply red — we'd seen reports of Canadian wildfire smoke drifting south, and whatever was in that atmosphere turned the sunset into something absolutely otherworldly. The sun looked like a glowing orange coal just hanging above the water, and its reflection stretched all the way across the marina in this brilliant rippling path of fire. I took picture after picture and still don't feel like I quite captured it. Some things you just have to see with your own eyes.

What a day, Petoskey. We'll be back.
