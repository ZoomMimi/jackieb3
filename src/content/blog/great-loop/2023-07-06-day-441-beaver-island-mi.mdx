What a day on Beaver Island! I have to say, this little island in Lake Michigan keeps surprising us. We woke up to find we had an overnight guest clinging to the boat — a absolutely enormous moth had made himself right at home on the Jackie B III. I mean this thing was huge. I'm not going to pretend I wasn't a little startled when I spotted him. Mike just laughed at me, of course.

Then, not long after, we watched something you don't see every day — a boat being hauled right down the street on a trailer. Just cruising along on dry land like it was the most normal thing in the world. We had to stop and watch that little parade go by. Only on Beaver Island!

We also came across a gorgeous antique map on display — a 1755 French cartographer's rendering of *Partie Occidentale de la Nouvelle France ou du Canada*, showing the Great Lakes in beautiful hand-colored detail. Lac Superieur, Lac Huron, Lac Michigan, Lac Erie, Lac Ontario — all right there, mapped out nearly 270 years ago. Standing there looking at it, knowing we've traveled so many of those same waters ourselves, was really something. And to think we're back up in this part of the world again — last year's Georgian Bay leg left such an impression on us that we just had to make a return trip to Canadian waters this summer. Seeing this old map made that feel even more meaningful.

The highlight of the afternoon, though, was the beach. The water along the shore was the most incredible shade of turquoise and green — honestly it looked more like the Caribbean than the Great Lakes. There were kiteboarders out making it look effortless, soaring across the waves with their colorful kites catching the wind. A seagull even photobombed one of my shots, which I'm choosing to see as a sign of good taste. I could have watched them all afternoon — and honestly, I think I did.

What a gem of a place. Beaver Island, you have not disappointed.
