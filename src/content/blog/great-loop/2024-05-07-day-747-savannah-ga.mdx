What a day on the water! We left this morning in lovely weather — a comfortable 72 degrees with just a gentle southwest breeze — and made our way 87 miles up the ICW to Savannah. Six hours of beautiful Georgia coastal scenery, and honestly it never gets old.

The run up was full of eye candy. As we came through the sounds we spotted a shrimp boat working her nets with what looked like half the bird population of coastal Georgia riding along for the free meal. Pelicans and gulls absolutely covered her outriggers — I kept waiting for the whole thing to tip over. We also passed a gorgeous estate-style property along the shore, all manicured lawn and a distinctive pointed cupola peeking above the live oaks, flying a flag — the kind of place you photograph from the water and spend the next twenty minutes trying to look up on your phone.

Then came that spectacular cable-stayed bridge — the twin towers framing the sky as we approached. We watched it first from a distance, those graceful cables stretching over the marsh, and then got closer for a better shot. I never get tired of bridges like that. Mike probably gets tired of me photographing bridges like that, but he's a good sport.

We pulled into Thunderbolt Marina and got ourselves settled. Thunderbolt is just a few miles down the Wilmington River from historic Savannah, which worked out perfectly. Once the boat was squared away we headed into the city to explore. Mike and I took a selfie somewhere along the cobblestoned streets.

The evening, though — the evening was the real show. We ended up at a spot overlooking the Savannah River, up high with the bridge right in view, and the maritime parade just did not stop. The tanker *Overseas Long Beach* was the first one to drift past, enormous and rust-red along the waterline. Then came a massive container ship stacked so high with boxes it looked like someone had accidentally hit "load more" and couldn't stop. We held up our drinks and watched it slide by like it owned the river — because honestly, it kind of does.

And then, just as the light was getting that soft golden-pink quality, the *Stena Promise* came through — a big green Stena Proman tanker, hailing from Limassol, with a little tugboat nudging her along. We watched two ships pass each other in the river at dusk with the cable-stayed bridge glowing behind them and the Westin tower in the background. I kept saying "okay, one more photo" and Mike kept nodding, because what else are you going to do?

Savannah does not disappoint.
