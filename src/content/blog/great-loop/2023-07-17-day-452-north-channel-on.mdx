Canada has a way of making you feel very small in the best possible way. We were so excited to be back in the North Channel — this is a return visit for us, and honestly it might be even more beautiful than we remembered. The water was so still and dark green that every boat anchored around us had a perfect mirror image beneath it. I kept stopping to make sure I was looking at the real thing and not the reflection.

We tucked in among a little community of boats for the night — a couple of sailboats, a trawler, and us. Looking out from the Jackie B III's bow, the scene was almost unreal: masts pointing straight up and straight down at the same time, the forested hillside doubled in the water, and storm clouds just beginning to gather on the horizon. I'm so glad I had my camera out because I honestly could have stared at it all evening.

Earlier in the day we got the dinghy out and a couple of folks from the neighboring boats went ashore to explore the rocks. The Canadian Shield is something else — these big smooth sweeping slabs of granite tumbling right down to the water's edge, with tall pines somehow growing straight up out of cracks in the rock. We pulled the dinghy up and scrambled around for a bit. It's the kind of place that makes you feel like an explorer, even if your hiking shoes are a little too new for the terrain.

Mike and I eventually headed inland a bit and found a proper trail through the forest — tall cedars and ferns, a little hidden pond with lily pads, and what looked like a tiny waterfall tucked back in the trees. We stopped to take a few pictures of ourselves, which is always a bit of a comedy of errors when you're trying to hold a phone steady on an uneven rock. We managed a decent one by the water's edge, and then a group shot with one of the other boaters who had joined us on the trail. Good people make good adventures.

By the time we got back to the Jackie B III the water was absolutely glassy and the anchored boats looked like they were floating in midair. The seaplane I spotted earlier in the day had long since gone, but the image of it skimming past the tree line is one I won't forget. There is truly nothing like the North Channel.
