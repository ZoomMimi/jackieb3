What a day on Lake Michigan! We left Little Traverse Bay at 9:17 this morning and covered just over 100 nautical miles in about six and a half hours, arriving at Manistee Municipal Marina in the early evening. The water was sparkling and the sun was bright — I kept snapping photos of the Garmin trying to capture the depth sounder readings as we crossed the deeper water. Out in the middle of the lake the depth was showing over 500 feet in places, which never stops being a little humbling. Lake Michigan is no joke.

I have to back up a bit and tell you about the night before, because it set the tone. We had dinner at the Terrace Inn — a Michigan Historic Site right there in Bay View, which I had to photograph because the marker on the wall tells quite a story. It opened in 1911 as the newest and most modern hotel in the area and has been welcoming guests ever since. The dining room did not disappoint. I ordered a watermelon salad with candied pecans, arugula, and what I think was goat cheese, finished with a balsamic drizzle — it was absolutely beautiful and tasted even better than it looked. I may have taken an embarrassing number of photos of my salad. No regrets.

Of course, before we could enjoy any of that, Mike had been down in the engine compartment. Because that is just life on the Jackie B III. He was peering into the turbo on the Volvo Penta — I snapped a photo of him tucked in there with that focused look on his face that means either everything is fine or everything is very much not fine. Turns out it was fine, thankfully, and we left with both engines running happily.

Pulling into Manistee was lovely. We passed the white lighthouse on the pier — we actually got several shots of it from different angles as we came in, and then again as the sun was setting with the light shimmering on the water behind us. It is one of those classic Lake Michigan lighthouse views that makes you stop whatever you are doing and just look.

The marina itself was a pleasant surprise. The clubhouse was bright and nicely appointed with big windows looking out over the water, and there was even a little basket of Crayola coloring supplies on the table — perfect if you have grandchildren aboard, which I thought was a sweet touch. There were a couple of boaters already settled into the Adirondack chairs on the patio when we arrived, just relaxing and reading. That looked like exactly the right idea.

We also explored a bit of the town on foot and discovered a wonderful old wooden boardwalk tucked in under a canopy of trees — the kind of shaded staircase path that feels like it belongs in another century. Very charming. And if you needed one more reason to love this marina, the Wi-Fi password is "F1sh1ng!" — which made me smile the moment I saw it on the welcome sheet.

Before we headed north toward Canada — because yes, we are making our way back up to the North Channel for a return visit, and I cannot wait — the Garmin chart was showing us passing North Manitou Island and tracking up past the Leelanau Peninsula. Watching those depth numbers climb back up as we neared the Northport area was almost as entertaining as the open-lake crossing itself.

A big, beautiful day. One hundred miles of Michigan at its finest.
