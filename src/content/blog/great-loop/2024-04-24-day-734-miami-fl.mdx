We pulled away from Largo Sound just after 10:30 in the morning, and right away the water turned that crazy turquoise that still makes me stop and stare no matter how many times I see it. A city skyline was visible off in the haze as we made our way north — Miami sitting right there on the horizon like a mirage. Mike had the AGLCA burgee flying, and I have to say it looked pretty good with that backdrop.

The run up through Biscayne Bay was just gorgeous. The water was every shade of green and blue you can imagine, and the low mangrove shoreline to the west looked like it was practically floating. About 37 miles and just over three hours later, we tucked into Boca Chita Key.

I was not prepared for how charming this place is. The ornamental lighthouse on its little coral rock island right at the harbor entrance — I took approximately four hundred photos of it, which felt completely reasonable at the time. We learned from the National Park Service sign dockside that the whole island was purchased in 1937 by Mark Honeywell — yes, *that* Honeywell, founder of Honeywell Heating Controls. He built all the coral rock structures you see, including the lighthouse. Sadly his wife Olive died from a fall here in 1939, and he eventually sold the property in 1945. Forty years later it became part of Biscayne National Park. Such an interesting history for such a quiet little spot.

Mike and I walked the island and of course had to get a selfie in front of the lighthouse. We may have looked a little ridiculous craning our necks up at it. Worth it.

The Jackie B III looked absolutely beautiful tied up at the dock with the palm trees behind her — I'm not biased at all.

And then the sunset happened. That lighthouse silhouetted against a sky that went from gold to deep orange while the AGLCA flag caught the last of the breeze — honestly, I kept going back out to take just one more photo. This is one of those nights on the Loop that I know I'll be talking about for years.

"From the rising of the sun to the place where it sets, the name of the Lord is to be praised."
