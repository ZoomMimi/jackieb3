Back in Canada again — and the North Channel is every bit as lovely as we remembered from last time through. Today was barely a boating day at all (a tenth of a mile doesn't exactly qualify as a passage!), but it was a full day in every other sense of the word.

The morning started with what I can only describe as a Looper love fest. A group of us gathered in what appeared to be a clubhouse or community room, and the walls told the whole story — burgees everywhere you looked, dozens of them, every yacht club and marina you could imagine represented on those walls. There were charts and paintings and all manner of nautical art mixed in too. My husband was deep in conversation over a tablet, and I was perfectly content sitting there taking it all in with my coffee. There is something so wonderful about being in a room full of people who completely understand why you sold your sensible life to go live on a boat.

One of the highlights of the day was getting to admire *Miss Lily* up close at the dock — a gorgeous trawler in blue and white with beautiful scrollwork along her hull. She is a real head-turner. I had to get a photo standing beside her. Overcast skies and all, she looked like a painting.

After the gathering we laced up our shoes and hit the trail with a couple of our fellow boaters. The forest up here in the North Channel is something else — tall pines, mossy rocks, roots and boulders on the path that kept us honest. My husband had his trekking poles working overtime, which was wise because the trail got a bit rugged in spots. (I, of course, was navigating on pure determination and questionable footwear choices.) We made it up to a rocky outcrop overlooking the most stunning still water below, pine trees framing the whole scene. We took approximately four hundred selfies because honestly, how do you not?

The smiles in those photos say everything. What a gift it is to be back here.
