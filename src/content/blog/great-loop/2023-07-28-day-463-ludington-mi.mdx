We left Manistee Lake at half past eight on a beautiful Friday morning, the air still cool and the wind barely a whisper out of the west. By the time we tied up six hours and 62 miles later, the temperature had climbed to a gorgeous 88 degrees. Not a bad way to spend a day on Lake Michigan, I have to say.

The run north along the Michigan coastline was just stunning. Lake Michigan was showing off — deep blue water sparkling in the sunshine, with just enough of a chop to keep things interesting but nothing that had me white-knuckling anything. I did manage to get a few photos from the flybridge, which I'm fairly proud of even if I did have to hold on with one hand. You can see in one of the shots that there was another boat out on the water making the same crossing — we were definitely not the only ones taking advantage of a perfect summer day.

The shoreline along this stretch of Michigan is something else. Those sand dunes are massive — great rolling hills of sand tucked up against the tree line, and the beach stretching out along the water's edge as far as you can see. We passed a lighthouse along the way that was just beautiful — tall, black and white, standing out against that sandy point like it had been painted there. I could have taken a hundred pictures.

Once we got to White Lake and tied up at the dock, Mike got busy with the lines while I took stock of the marina's whiteboard. The dockhands — Zack and Neva, according to the board — had done a great job posting the weather: air temp 88°F, water temp 70°F, winds 7 mph, waves 2 to 3 feet. Pretty much exactly what we experienced! It's always nice when the forecast matches reality.

After we got settled, we had the chance to walk over to a sweet little lighthouse tucked right in among the trees near the dock — a stone keeper's cottage with a small black iron lantern on top, all surrounded by big leafy trees. It had such a different character from the tall tower we'd seen from the water earlier in the day. I love how every lighthouse on these Great Lakes seems to have its own personality.

And then there was the downtown evening stroll, which produced my favorite photo of the day — and probably one of my favorites from the whole trip. I caught the reflection of the main street in a puddle, with the neon sign of the Vogue Theatre glowing in red and the streetlamps doubling themselves in the water. It looked like something out of a dream.

It is so good to be back in this part of the Great Lakes. We were here during the Loop, and returning feels like visiting an old friend — familiar and wonderful all at once.
