What a day! We pulled into Fernandina Beach after a beautiful 52-mile run up the ICW, and I'm not sure we could have timed it better if we'd tried. The water was a little choppy in spots — you can see in the wake shots that it was lively out there — but the sky was blue and the scenery kept us entertained the whole way. We passed a row of Navy destroyers lined up along the shore that stopped us both in our tracks. Just impressive. Then a massive tanker came into view that dwarfed everything around it. Mike and I just looked at each other. The Jackie B III is a fine boat, but she is definitely not that.

We had friends joining us for this stop, and what a stop to pick! We had no idea when we planned it that we'd be sailing right into the Isle of Eight Flags Shrimp Festival. The whole waterfront was buzzing. Decorated shrimp boats were parading right past us — the *Kimberly Christine* all decked out in red, white, and bunting; the *Miss Peggy* in her bold red hull; the *Native Sons*; others I could barely read fast enough as they went by. Each one was covered stem to stern in flags, streamers, and people waving like crazy. We were waving back from the dock like tourists. Which, fair enough, we are.

There was a bagpiper leading a procession along the waterfront — full kilt, full pipes — with a nun and an altar server right behind him. Only at a shrimp festival! There were custom golf carts on display, pirate floats, and all the festival energy you'd expect from a town that takes its shrimp seriously. Mike posed in front of a pirate ship float and looked right at home, I have to say.

We also got a chance to get our toes in the sand at the beach nearby.

And then the evening — oh, the evening. We had dinner with another couple under the most wonderful tiki hut roof right on the water. The whole place was open-air, with the lights of the marina reflecting off the water behind us. Good food, good company, cold drinks. Exactly what you want after a great day on the water.
