What a way to spend the Fourth of July! We woke up early — I mean *early*, engines running just after 5:30 in the morning — and headed out across Lake Michigan toward Beaver Island. The water was calm and glassy in the predawn light, and honestly it was one of those mornings where you can't quite believe this is your life. Mike was at the helm looking very pleased with himself, drink in hand, those big sanddunes sliding by off the stern — I got a great shot of him with that golden dune behind him that just about sums up the whole morning.

Along the way we passed a rusting shipwreck beached against a sandy bluff, its old hull absolutely covered in cormorants. The evening light painted it orange and it looked almost beautiful in a haunting sort of way. One of those sights that makes you reach for your camera.

After about 6½ hours and 55 miles we arrived at the Beaver Island Municipal Dock — and yes, I did photograph the wifi sign. "BI Municipal Dock / Password: DiscoverBI." You're welcome. 😄

And then the parade! We had no idea we were pulling in on the Fourth of July in time for Beaver Island's celebration, and it was the most charming small-town parade you can imagine. Floats from "Cadys of Sand Bay" wishing everyone a Happy 4th, a potter from St. James Pottery Studio actually throwing clay *on the moving trailer*, a little red VW Bug carrying the "Emerald Isle Irish Féile" contingent with an Irish flag draped over the side, a wild homemade contraption with a sign that read "Hearing Protection Required" (and I believed it), classic Corvettes in every color, and a sleek new mid-engine Corvette that got plenty of appreciative looks from the crowd. The whole island turned out — chairs lined up along the street, red-white-and-blue everywhere.

We ended the evening anchored out, the Jackie B III glowing in the most spectacular sunset I've photographed in a long time. Three photos, each one more golden than the last, with the boat perfectly silhouetted against the tree line. Then the full moon rose — enormous and orange — and topped the whole thing off. As if the sunset through the rain-streaked window wasn't already dramatic enough. I may have taken one too many sunset photos. I definitely did not stop.

Happy 4th of July from Beaver Island, Michigan. What a day.
