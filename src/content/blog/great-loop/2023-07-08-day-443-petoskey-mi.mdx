What a lovely little day on the water. We only traveled 3.5 nautical miles — barely enough time to finish a cup of coffee — but sometimes the short days are the sweetest ones. We slipped out of our spot on Little Traverse Bay just after 11:30 and were tucked into Petoskey before lunchtime. The wind was barely a whisper out of the west and the temperature was absolutely perfect. I don't think Mike and I said more than a handful of words the whole crossing — we just soaked it in.

Now, before I even talk about where we ended up, I have to tell you about what we spotted on the water. A mama merganser was out with her little ones — and she had quite a brood keeping up with her! I grabbed my camera and tried not to shriek with delight as she herded those fuzzy little ducklings along. I took about a dozen pictures because I couldn't decide which one was best. (Spoiler: none of them are good enough to capture how adorable the whole scene was, but I included them anyway.)

The evening was just gorgeous. Mike and I grabbed a few selfies on the back deck as the sun started to drop — those clouds behind us were putting on quite a show. I realize we take a lot of selfies. Some habits are hard to break.

One of the real highlights was watching a stunning classic wooden yacht called the *Aphrodite* glide right past us in the harbor. That teak and white hull was absolutely breathtaking — the kind of boat that makes you stop mid-sentence and just stare. I could not stop snapping photos of her.

The reflections of the boats and buildings in the still water actually looked beautiful.

It's wonderful to be back in this part of the world. We fell in love with the Great Lakes and Canadian waters on our Loop, and this little return visit to the North Channel area has us smiling all over again. Petoskey is a great jumping-off point for what's ahead, and we couldn't have asked for a more peaceful arrival.

"He stilled the storm to a whisper; the waves of the sea were hushed."
