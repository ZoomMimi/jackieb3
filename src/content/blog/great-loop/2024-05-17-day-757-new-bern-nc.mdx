What a day. What a journey. After 757 days and more miles than I can quite wrap my head around, we pulled the Jackie B III into New Bern, North Carolina — and just like that, the Great Loop was complete.

We left the dock around 9:53 in the morning under partly cloudy skies, temperatures in the low 70s and a light breeze out of the east — honestly about as pleasant as you could ask for on a travel day. The run up to New Bern was about 40 nautical miles through the ICW, all marshes and sandy points and quiet stretches of water with cypress trees lining the banks. I kept sneaking looks behind us at the wake curling out and thinking, *this is it. This is the last run.* Mike just kept his eyes forward and drove. That's Mike.

A day or two before we arrived, I had walked around town a bit and fallen completely in love with the wildflowers. Someone — a very wise someone — had let the front yard of one of the old white antebellum homes go absolutely wild with yellow coreopsis. It was a sea of gold right up to the front walk. I must have taken fifteen photos. I could not stop. I am not sorry.

And then there was the sunset the night before we crossed the finish. That bridge, that sky — I don't have words that are good enough. Orange and red and gold stacked up to the clouds, with our AGLCA burgee right there in the foreground. I cried a little. Mike did not. (Though I have my suspicions.)

When we came through the drawbridge into the marina, there were people on the dock waiting for us in the rain. That part got me too. Friends had made the drive to be there, huddled under umbrellas — one bright red-and-white one that was hard to miss — just to wave us in. We all crammed onto the Jackie B III with chips and dips and drinks and laughed and hugged and talked over each other the way you do when everyone is just so happy to be in the same place. The salon windows were spotted with rain and nobody cared one bit.

I am wearing my Jackie B III shirt in every photo and I have zero regrets about that either.

We did it.
