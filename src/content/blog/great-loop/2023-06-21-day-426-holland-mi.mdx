We only moved 4.9 nautical miles today — barely enough to call it a travel day — but what a way to end it. We're back in the Great Lakes on our return visit to Canadian waters and the North Channel, and right now we find ourselves in Holland, Michigan, soaking up every bit of summer this side of the border has to offer before we head north again.

The evening completely stole the show. Mike and I settled in to watch the sunset from the water, and I have to say — I've seen a lot of sunsets on this trip, but nothing quite like this one. The smoke haze in the air (from the Canadian wildfires that have been drifting down over the Great Lakes) turned the sun into the most vivid, perfectly round ball of red I have ever seen. It literally looked like a glowing red orb just hovering above the horizon. I took about a hundred photos trying to capture it, and honestly every single one of them looks unreal — like someone digitally added a red circle to the sky. But that's exactly what it looked like with our own eyes.

We weren't the only ones who noticed. The pier was absolutely packed with people, all of them stopped in their tracks, phones out, just watching. There's something wonderful about a sunset that brings a whole crowd of strangers together like that.

We snapped a selfie with the whole pier and that peachy sky behind us. 😄

Back at the marina, the sun finally sank behind the boats at the dock and the sky turned the most beautiful shade of peach and rose. One of our dock neighbors, *Je t'aime*, was perfectly silhouetted against it all. Even the masts were glowing.

What a gift of an evening.

"From the rising of the sun to the place where it sets, the name of the Lord is to be praised."
