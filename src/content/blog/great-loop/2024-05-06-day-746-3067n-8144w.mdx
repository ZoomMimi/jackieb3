We had some wonderful visitors join us for part of this stretch of the Loop, and I have to say they were great sports about all of it! We all had a fabulous time together — dinner out, laughing around the table, the works. You can see in those first photos that everyone was smiling. That's the kind of crew you want aboard!

But the real highlight of our time here at Amelia Island? The sunsets. I cannot overstate this. Mike and I pulled on our Amelia Island Sea Turtle Watch shirts — yes, we are those people — and walked down to the beach, and what we got was absolutely stunning. The sun dipped toward the horizon, the clouds caught fire in orange and pink, and the waves just kept rolling in like they had all the time in the world. We took about a hundred photos trying to capture it. I think we got close!

The next morning we were underway by just after 9 a.m. and headed north through Cumberland Sound. The water was calm, the wind was light out of the south, and it was just one of those perfectly pleasant days on the water. Somewhere along the way we spotted a submarine tied up at a dock — just sitting there, big as life, with a crane working away on it. You don't see that every day from the helm of a 43-foot trawler!

We also got a beautiful view of a lighthouse peeking up over the dunes from the water — that wild stretch of sandy coast with the dunes stacked up behind it was just gorgeous. Everything feels a little grander when you're seeing it from the water.

On the island itself, Mike and I made it out to what can only be described as one of the most otherworldly beaches I've ever walked on — acres of bleached, twisted driftwood trees tumbled right down to the water's edge, with rocks piled around their roots. It's eerie and beautiful all at once, and honestly the photos don't even do it justice. We took several selfies trying to get the composition right and mostly just ended up looking sunburned and happy.

The roads through the island were another story entirely — live oaks draped in Spanish moss arching completely over the lane, like something out of a dream. I kept wanting to stop the car every thirty seconds. Mike was very patient with me. Mostly.

"He made everything beautiful in its time."
