Back in Canada again, and honestly it feels like coming home. The North Channel has a way of doing that to you — once it gets its hooks in, you just have to come back. We covered about 20 miles in just a couple of hours under a brilliant blue sky, with the water that gorgeous shade of green-blue that honestly doesn't look real until you're sitting right on top of it.

One of the highlights of the morning run was spotting a sweet little lighthouse perched up on the pink granite rocks — very Canadian, very North Channel. Mike and I snapped about a dozen selfies trying to get it framed just right behind us. I think we took the same shot three times and declared each one "the best one." You can judge for yourself.

As we made our way along, we passed some absolutely jaw-dropping properties along the shoreline. There were these massive log and stone lodges sitting right up on the rocky outcroppings above the water — the kind of places that make you say "okay, who lives like this?" The water was so clear you could practically see straight to the bottom, and the blue of the sky against those pink Canadian Shield rocks was just perfection. One property even had what looked like a giant wooden canoe as a roofline feature, with a pair of red Adirondack chairs sitting out front. Very on brand for Ontario, I must say.

We also had some wonderful friends join us on the Jackie B III for part of the day — two couples who clearly know how to make an adventure out of anything. We had gotten together earlier at a beautifully built log lodge with a stone fireplace porch — very rustic-chic — and the company and laughter were just as good as the scenery. Somebody brought out a box of treats that had to be the most Canadian birthday "cake" I've ever seen: a butter tart sitting on top of a chocolate brownie, surrounded by more butter tarts, with a single birthday candle stuck right in the middle of it. I mean, when in Canada. We laughed until we cried lighting that little candle.

Later in the day we gathered with a great group of fellow Loopers at the Split Rail Brewing Co. on Manitoulin Island — you could see the sign right on the wall and that name just fits the place perfectly. It had that great rustic bar feel, wood-paneled walls covered in photos, and a real sense of community. Dean and Lorri — wearing their matching stars-and-stripes Warrior Sailing shirts and their "Incognito" name tags, which cracked everyone up — gave a lively presentation to the group. Those two are a lot of fun. Mike and I also got to meet up with Peter and his wife, which was a real treat. Good people, good stories, good laughs all around.

Days like this one are exactly why we came back to the North Channel. There is truly no place like it.
