We left Cocoa Village Marina this morning with blue skies overhead and a great wake behind us. Looking back as we pulled away, you could already see the city skyline fading in the distance — one of those bittersweet views that reminds you this Loop keeps moving whether you're ready or not!

Our first stop was Titusville, and the run up the ICW did not disappoint. We passed under a bridge that had a full construction barge set up alongside it — cranes towering overhead, the whole operation. It's always a little surreal to cruise right through an active construction zone like that. We gave it plenty of room!

Then came the stretch that honestly might be one of the most unique on the entire Loop. Passing Kennedy Space Center from the water is something else. The Vehicle Assembly Building — that massive white block of a structure — just dominates the horizon. I kept grabbing my camera trying to get a decent shot across the water. The launch pads were visible too, off in the haze to the left. I kept thinking about all the history over on that little strip of land and here we are just floating right by it in the Jackie B III. Pretty humbling, actually.

The shoreline along that stretch is beautifully wild — low mangrove islands, sandy little spits, not a building in sight on that side. Just birds and water and space infrastructure. Only in Florida!

After we tied up in Titusville mid-morning, Mike and I made our way over to the beach for a bit. The sky was that perfect deep blue, the waves were rolling in, and someone was flying a big red kite surfer canopy up above the beach. I may have taken a slightly sideways selfie — okay fine, completely upside down — but I'm grinning, so that counts as a win.

We were back underway by early afternoon and headed north, covering a good stretch of the ICW before calling it a day. The afternoon light over those little wooded islands was just gorgeous.

But the real show was the sunset. I don't have words good enough for it. The sun tucked behind a big thunderhead cloud and shot rays out in every direction — gold, orange, deep amber — across the whole sky. We took about a hundred photos and I'm not sure any of them truly capture it. God was showing off tonight, that's for sure.

"From the rising of the sun to the place where it sets, the name of the Lord is to be praised."
Psalms 113:3 NIV
