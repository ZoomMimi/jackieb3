We got an early start — out of Charleston Harbor just after 6:30 in the morning — which meant the sky was doing something spectacular before we'd even had a second cup of coffee. I don't know how many sunrises we've watched from this boat at this point, but they still stop me in my tracks every single time. These were particularly good ones. The clouds were layered in that way that turns the whole sky into something you feel like you should be paying admission to see.

Right as we were leaving the harbor, we passed Fort Sumter sitting out there in the water like it always has, stoic and rust-red and a little bit haunting. I never get tired of seeing it. There's something about passing a place where history actually happened — where the first shots of the Civil War were fired — that makes me put down my coffee and just look. We took a few passes at it with the camera. Mike kept the headset on and the charts up, because even in a beautiful moment, somebody has to drive the boat.

Speaking of Mike — I love that man, but I especially love him when he's framed by a glowing orange sunrise behind the helm with that headset on, looking like he absolutely knows what he's doing. Which he does. Most of the time. 😊

A little further along we caught a barge working its way through the channel — massive and low in the water, the kind of thing that reminds you very quickly that you are the smaller vessel in this situation. We gave it plenty of room.

The real scene-stealer of the morning, though, was a shrimp trawler heading out just as the sun was breaking the horizon. The nets were rigged, the light was pure gold, and a channel marker stood right in the foreground. I took about fifteen photos trying to capture it. A few of them actually worked.

We made a fuel stop at Wacca Wache Marina midday, which was a good excuse to stretch our legs before heading back out for the afternoon run. By the time we tied up for the night we'd covered 110 miles in about eight and a half hours — a solid day by anyone's measure.

Along the way, we also spotted what looked like the *Irish Rover* out of Occoquan, VA tucked in at the dock. Always fun to see the hometowns on the transoms — you never know where fellow boaters are coming from.

And tucked along the shore somewhere in that afternoon stretch, a lighthouse rose up behind a sandy beach and a wall of dark green pines. No fanfare, just quietly beautiful. That's been this whole journey, really.
