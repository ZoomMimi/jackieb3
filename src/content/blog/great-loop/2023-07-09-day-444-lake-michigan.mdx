We left Little Traverse Bay around 10 in the morning under a beautiful blue sky, and I have to say the water color along that stretch of Lake Michigan just never gets old. The shoreline as we pulled away was absolutely stunning — grand old summer cottages with wraparound porches lined up along white sandy beaches, the kind of homes you imagine have been in families for generations. I kept grabbing my camera every few minutes, which I'm sure Mike found very efficient of me.

![](photo1)

The water was that gorgeous deep blue that makes you feel like you're out on a proper ocean crossing, not a freshwater lake. As we moved along, a curious stone lighthouse came into view — sitting all by itself out in the water, looking more than a little worse for wear. It had a certain dramatic, crumbling grandeur about it that I thought was wonderful, even if it's clearly seen better days.

![](photo7)

Then, little by little, the Mackinac Bridge began to appear on the horizon. I never get tired of that sight. It started as a thin line against the blue sky, and then grew and grew until we were passing right underneath it. Looking straight up from the boat through those enormous green steel trusses is something else entirely — it feels like the whole structure is about to come right down on top of you. In the best possible way, of course.

![](photo11)

I should mention — this is our return trip to these waters. We came through here during our Great Loop last year, but honestly getting to experience the Straits of Mackinac by boat a second time is just as spectacular as the first. Some things simply don't get ordinary.

We docked at the Mackinac Island State Dock and promptly made our way to the Pink Pony — celebrating its 75th anniversary this season, which felt like a very good reason to order a pink sparkling cocktail. The bar opens right up onto the harbor, and the view through those bottles lined up on the ledge, with the ferry coming and going in the background, was just perfect. I did not complain.

![](photo16)

We also found our way to some snacks — a very good dip situation with chips and crackers that went perfectly with a Long Drink. After the boat life on the water all day, it felt exactly right.

![](photo17)

And of course, there's no missing the horse-drawn taxi making its rounds on the streets of Mackinac Island. No cars here — just horses, bikes, and the smell of fudge wafting through the air. A good day all around.

![](photo18)
