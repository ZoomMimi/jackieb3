We are back in Canada, and I have to say — it feels like coming home to a place that was never quite ours to keep. The North Channel did not disappoint last time, and it certainly has not disappointed now. If anything, knowing what was waiting for us made the anticipation even sweeter.

We found ourselves tucked into a gorgeous anchorage surrounded by the Canadian Shield at its most dramatic — those great grey-pink slabs of granite rising straight up out of the water, draped with pines clinging on for dear life. The water here is that impossible shade of teal-green that you almost don't believe is real until you're actually floating on it. Early in the morning the whole cove was like a mirror. I could not stop taking pictures, because every angle looked like a painting. The SUP boards came off the back of the Jackie B III and floated alongside us, which honestly just added to the scene.

Mike, of course, could not resist scrambling up the rocks to see what the view looked like from above — and the answer is: absolutely spectacular. Looking straight down from the cliff you could see the Jackie B III sitting there like a little white toy boat in an emerald bowl. There were sailboats on moorings all around us, and the whole cove was just full of happy boaters doing exactly what we were doing — not much of anything, and loving every minute of it.

We ended up hiking up to the top of the ridge, which I will admit required a little more athleticism than I personally had budgeted for. I was very grateful for good shoes. From up there you get a panoramic view that sweeps from the anchorage all the way out to the open North Channel — it is the kind of view that makes you feel very small and very grateful at the same time. Mike and a couple of our hiking companions made it look easy. I made it look like a project. Worth it either way.

The afternoon light on that turquoise water, with the boats reflected perfectly below the cliffs — I honestly do not have words big enough for it. Canada, you have done it again.

"He makes me lie down in green pastures, he leads me beside quiet waters."
