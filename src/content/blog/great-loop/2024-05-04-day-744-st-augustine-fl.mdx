What a beautiful morning to be on the water! We pulled away just after 10:30 and pointed the Jackie B III north, covering 23.2 nautical miles up the ICW to St. Augustine. The weather was perfect — warm, a light breeze out of the ESE, and just enough clouds to keep things interesting.

It didn't take long before the scenery started doing all the talking. We passed a old stone fort sitting right at the water's edge — it looked like something out of another century, which I suppose it is. Sailboats bobbed nearby and a little beach crowd had gathered around it. Such a striking sight from the water.

As we continued north, a black-and-white spiral lighthouse peeked up above the treeline. I love spotting those from the boat — there's something about lighthouses that just never gets old.

Then came St. Augustine herself, and honestly, the approach from the water might be the best way to see this city. The waterfront with all those beautiful red-roofed buildings stacked up behind the docks is just gorgeous. And the Bridge of Lions did not disappoint — those four red-capped towers are so elegant, I could look at that bridge all day. We watched another boat pass under it just ahead of us.

Right in the channel we spotted the shrimp trawler *Capt Trey* heading out with her nets spread wide. I always get a kick out of the working boats — they don't care one bit about the scenery.

Once we were docked at 57 Comares Ave, the marina handed us the all-important card with the gate code (359), bathroom code (241), and wifi password. Marina check-in in a nutshell!

We ended the afternoon at the Conch House Marina Resort — a fun spot with a pool and a tropical vibe. We met up with another couple and the four of us sat outside enjoying the sunshine. St. Augustine is such a special place. We've barely scratched the surface and I'm already a fan.
